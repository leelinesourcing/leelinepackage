## How We Shortlisted These Manufacturers?

Four criteria, weighted in this order. The weights are editorial judgment, not a published index, and the list runs roughly by company size rather than by fit for a small buyer — the table above is the honest comparison.

1. **Published small-order policy — 35%.** Does the company publish a minimum order, a self-serve portal, or a named program a small buyer can actually use? A published policy beats a press release about flexibility.
2. **Customization depth — 25%.** Can it take your own dieline, print and finish, or only configure a catalog item?
3. **Sustainability evidence — 20%.** Certified fiber, recycled content and published reporting, rather than a landing page.
4. **Service footprint — 20%.** Plants, local teams, and whether lead times are stated publicly.

Where a company does not publish an MOQ, this page says so instead of estimating one.

## When a US Manufacturer Is the Wrong Choice?

Most of the frustration small buyers report comes from five situations. Check them before you send a brief to any of the ten.

- **You need fewer than a few hundred units.** None of the ten advertises a program built for that volume. Below it you are paying for a setup, not a product.
- **Your dieline does not exist yet.** These companies convert packaging. Structural design and prototyping for a new format is usually a separate service, and some will not quote until you supply a tested die.
- **You need a short run of fully custom print.** Digital print makes a 500–2,000-unit run possible at a converter, not at a plant tooled for a retail program.
- **Your deadline is measured in days.** Samples and tooling slots sit behind larger accounts at most of these plants.
- **You need a rigid gift box, a printed mailer or hand-assembled packaging.** Several of the ten do not make those formats at all — check the "Not a fit" line under each entry.
