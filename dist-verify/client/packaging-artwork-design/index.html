Why it pays

Turn Packaging Into Your Brand’s Secret Weapon

In a crowded market the pack is the first touchpoint — what it says, how it opens and what it costs to ship decide whether a product is picked up, kept and reordered.

Stand out on shelves

A shelf is won in a second. A pack that is unmistakable stops the eye, stands apart from the one beside it and makes the product easy to find again — so the brand is remembered, not just seen.

Enhance the unboxing

The open is part of the product. A considered reveal — the weight, the moment, the detail inside — turns a delivery into something worth photographing, and a first-time buyer into a regular who tells others.

Drive business growth

Better packaging pays for itself. A tighter, well-designed pack usually ships cheaper, damages less and reads more premium — lifting perceived value, repeat purchase and word of mouth over the life of a brand.

3 · File spec

A File That Prints First Time

Most ‘looks right on screen’ files fail on the press. These are the specs that keep a file out of the rework pile.

A supplier needs the dieline, the bleed and the separations to match — and the key visual, the codes and the inside print in the right zones. Hand over a file that already accounts for them and the first proof is the one approved.

  • PDF/X-1a or an unflattened AI, art on its own layer
  • Text outlined and fonts embedded
  • Images at print resolution, at 100% scale
  • Finishes and spot colours on their own layers
  • Inside-print and unboxing detail on the keyline layer
  • Dieline locked to a keyline layer that can be hidden

4 · Colour

Hold the Brand Colour Across Every Run

CMYK drifts between presses. Pantone holds. Here is how the brand colour stays the same on the first box and the ten-thousandth.

5 · Craft × structure

Where Artwork Meets the Structure

A finish can be ruined by the fold it lands on. A few rules keep the artwork and the box agreeing — the rest is structural engineering.

Do Place for a clean fold

  • Keep large foil areas off crease lines — a fold through a wide foil cracks it.
  • Hold the key logo off a window and off the die-cut edge.
  • Route a large flat colour area around a fold instead of straight across it.
  • Give a panel that tucks a little extra margin, so the fold never eats the art.

Avoid The classic failures

  • Don’t run fine type in a spot colour alone — thin type drops out on a rough board.
  • Don’t trap a solid dark area against a fold — it scuffs and shows a line.
  • Don’t place a QR where a fold or a coating warps it.
  • Don’t split an element that must line up across two panels that fold apart.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

The practical questions before a project starts. Structure and sample detail live on their own pages.

QWhat do we need from you to get started?

A brand manual, a logo, a high-res export or a printed pack — plus rough dimensions and a quantity. That is enough; anything on top is redrawn to vector here.

QCan you fix artwork that has already been made?

Usually. The logo and headline are redrawn to vector and the layout rebuilt to the dieline, then a print-feasibility check shows what is fixable before committing.

QHow long does the artwork design take?

A first proof usually lands within a few days, and small revisions within a couple of days. The timeline depends on how much needs redrawing, not on the run size.

QDo you work from our brand guidelines, or create the brand too?

Work starts from existing brand guidelines and artwork. With only a logo or a rough idea, the packaging artwork is drawn around it — the broader brand identity stays separate.

QHow is the quote structured?

A one-off setup plus any plates or tooling the finish needs, then a per-unit price that falls as quantity rises. The quote lists each line, so the total is clear before committing.

QWho owns the artwork and the dieline, and is it kept confidential?

Ownership stays with the buyer. The artwork and dieline are the buyer’s files, a master is kept for reorders, and work is held under NDA on request.
