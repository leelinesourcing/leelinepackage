The catalogue, and why each exists

Nine Gift Box Types

You are not shopping for a shape. You are choosing the movement a customer makes when the box reaches them: a lid that lifts, a tray that slides, a panel that shows the product without opening anything.

  • Custom Rigid Gift Boxes

    1.5–3 mm board

    Heaviest of the nine. A wrapped board box that keeps its corners and carries a molded tray — the build a keepsake gift is expected to arrive in.

  • Magnetic Closure Gift Boxes

    N35–N52 magnets

    Two magnets buried under the wrap. It closes with a resistance a hand reads before a word is read, and the lid stays shut in a bag on the way home.

  • Drawer Gift Boxes

    sleeve and tray

    Slides out rather than lifting, so nothing tips on the way to the customer’s hands.

  • Folding Gift Boxes

    ships flat

    Stored and freighted flat, then assembled by hand. The lowest unit cost and the smallest footprint in a stockroom.

  • Window Gift Boxes

    PET panel

    A clear panel in the lid. The product is visible before the box is opened, which is the question a shelf asks.

  • Corporate Gift Boxes

    repeat runs

    Built for repetition: a single artwork, hundreds of identical boxes, and a delivery date that does not move.

  • Lid and Base Gift Boxes

    deep base

    The classic two-piece. A deep base holds the product low, and the whole lid lifts away to show a printed inside.

  • Two-Piece Gift Boxes

    equal halves

    Half the height of a lid-and-base, both halves wrapped from a single sheet so the pair reads as one object.

  • Eco-Friendly Gift Boxes

    kraft, no plastic

    Unbleached kraft, recycled board and a paper tie, with soy-based inks that cut VOC emissions by up to 25 %.

Board and paper

The Two Materials That Decide the Price

Neither of these appears in your artwork, and both move the number further than the print does. A heavier board survives a shelf and adds freight to every unit; a cheaper wrap saves at the die and scuffs in the first customer’s hands.

  • Greyboard

    1.5–3 mm

    Keeps a lid square and survives a stack, so the box still feels like a gift after a pallet has sat on it — and every extra millimeter is weight you pay freight on.

  • Fluted Board

    E-flute

    Same stiffness as a solid board, less weight, because the air trapped in the flute does the work. Lower freight for a parcel that ships in its own carton.

  • Recycled Kraft

    20–30 % cheaper

    The cheapest ground here, 20–30 % under a rigid build, and printable in a single color or two so a one-color mark looks deliberate.

  • Printed Wrap

    300–400 gsm

    What the hand reads first. Paper weight is what makes a printed box feel considered instead of flimsy before anyone opens it.

Two of these are not what you think

Gift Box, Rigid Box, Jewelry Box

“A gift box with a ring slot” names a purpose and a cut in the same sentence, and it comes back quoted twice. Naming the object before the brief is written is the cheapest decision on this page.

  • A gift box is a purpose

    Sized to an occasion rather than to a pallet, with the cavity left open for tissue and a tie. What gets priced is a structure, a wrap and a tray taken together — and every box on this page is quoted that way.

  • A rigid box is a construction

    Greyboard wrapped in paper, built wherever a lid has to keep its shape. The same build serves a gift, a keepsake or a plain shelf carton with no tray at all — the construction is not the job. See how a rigid box is built.

  • A jewelry box is a rigid box with a cut insert

    The board is identical. What changes is the tray: wells cut to hold a ring or a chain in place. Where a box needs slots rather than a tray, the wells are cut and priced separately. See the jewelry cut.

Told it is included, then it is not

What Else Comes With the Box

Three things are treated as free by almost every buyer and as separate operations by every factory. They are the difference between the number approved and the invoice that arrives.

  1. 01

    Printing Inside the Lid

    The first surface a customer reads once the lid is off, and it is priced as its own print. Specify it in the brief and it prints with the wrap; ask for it once the dieline is cut and it needs its own board and its own press pass.

  2. 02

    A Tray Cut to Your Product

    Cut to the packed length, width and height of the product itself rather than to its category. A tray holds whatever settles into it; a jewelry well pins a piece in place, and the two are not interchangeable.

  3. 03

    The Chip That Matches Run Two

    Filed against the run are the retained wrap chip and the dieline, so a second order is matched to the first instead of rematched by eye — the whole difference between two runs that agree and two that nearly do.

The part hands meet first

The Tie, and the Three That Get Quoted

The tie is the first thing a customer’s hands touch and the last thing most briefs specify. Widths run 2–4 mm for twine and 9–25 mm for ribbon, and all three take a printed or hot-stamped mark.

  • Satin

    flat sheen

    A flat sheen that photographs well and takes a hot-stamped mark. It slips, which is why a satin tie wants a double knot or a seal.

  • Woven

    ribbed grip

    Ribbed, so it grips: it holds a knot without heat, resists a crease, and prints a matte mark cleanly.

  • Raffia and Twine

    natural fiber

    Natural fiber for a kraft box — the lowest-cost tie, and the one that reads handmade rather than luxury.

Count back from the date

How an Order Runs

Your launch date is fixed and everything else moves backwards from it. A printed sample has to exist before bulk starts and a container has to cross before the boxes reach a shelf, which leaves the date as the only decision that cannot be late.

  1. Day 1

    Brief

    Packed dimensions, the unit count, and the day the boxes are needed.

  2. Day 2

    3D Mockup

    You see the structure and the artwork on screen before anything is cut, which is what makes a change free at this point.

  3. Day 5–7

    White Dummy

    A size-only box, unprinted. This is where a product that does not fit the cavity is found — the cheapest place to find it.

  4. Day 12–17

    Printed Sample

    The real board, the real wrap and the real mark in your hands. Your approval here is what opens the bulk run.

  5. Week 3–5

    Bulk Run

    Board cut, wrapped, assembled, and inspected as the run moves rather than once the pallet is closed.

  6. Week 6–8

    Ship

    Four to six weeks by sea to the destination on the quote, with the paperwork sent ahead of the container.

A folding box travels and stores as a stack of boards. A rigid build travels at its own volume and is paid for along the way — worth knowing before the board gets thicker for a shelf that will never see it.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions that arrive after the first quote, answered with the numbers behind them.

What is the minimum order for a custom gift box?

500 units per size, per structure and per wrap. Volume tiers sit at 1,000, 5,000 and 10,000 units, and the unit price steps down at each rung. A second size is a second die, quoted as its own run.

What should I check on the printed sample?

Three things, and they are the three that cannot be fixed later: that the tray holds the product without pressing on it, that the lid closes with the same resistance the mockup showed, and that the mark sits where the dieline put it. A change at that point costs one more sample rather than a re-cut.

Do you need a print-ready file to start?

No. A logo and a colour reference are enough — the dieline is drawn to the box and the artwork is placed on it. A print-ready file skips one proof round, which is the only difference it makes to the schedule.

What happens if my product changes after the order?

The tray changes with it. The die, the board and the printed wrap belong to the box, so a new tray is a new cut rather than a new run — which is exactly why the tray is quoted on its own line.

Where is the box made?

In China, at a facility holding ISO 9001, ISO 14001 and FSC certification. Samples and bulk both ship to the destination named on the quote, with no third party in between.

Can a gift box be shipped on its own?

Two boxes do two jobs, and keeping them apart is the point. The gift box is built around the reveal — a wrapped rigid body on a cut tray, drop-tested at 1.2 m so it comes out of the master carton unmarked. The parcel around it is a corrugated carton, and that one takes the courier network on its own fluting and edge-crush specification. They are engineered against different tests.
