Nine ways to hold a product

Nine Corrugated Constructions, Cut Around Your Product

Each ships as a flat blank and folds into a carton on your side. The difference between them is what the product needs held: a bottle, a book, a shelf edge or a pallet.

  • Custom Corrugated Shipping Boxes

    32–48 ECT

    A carton cut to your product’s own length, width and depth, which is what stops a pallet carrying air that is charged as freight.

  • Corrugated Mailer Boxes

    Self-locking, no tape

    A wrap that folds around the contents and locks without tape, taking a parcel out of a gray bag and into printed board.

  • Die-Cut Corrugated Boxes

    Any aperture, same die

    A shaped front or a cut window pressed from the same die as the body. The aperture is part of the blank rather than a second operation.

  • Heavy Duty Corrugated Boxes

    Divided cells, die-cut handle

    Divided cells and a reinforced base for the carriers that end up on the bottom layer of a pallet with everything else above them.

  • Custom Printed Corrugated Boxes

    Flexo, spot or full color

    Full-panel print on kraft liner, holding registration on a board face that is not flat — which is where most corrugated print fails.

  • Wholesale Corrugated Boxes

    500 – 500,000 units

    The same specification held across a long run, with the unit rate stepping down at the volume tier the order actually reaches.

  • Double Wall Corrugated Boxes

    BC and EB, 6.4 mm

    Two flutes bonded, for a lid-and-base box that keeps its shape under a stack and still reads as a gift.

  • Custom Corrugated Packaging Boxes

    Roll end tuck top

    A roll-end assembly that folds up from the flat blank on the packing bench, with no glue line waiting to fail in transit.

  • Tab-Lock Corrugated Boxes

    No glue, no staples

    Boxes that assemble by folding and unfold the same way, which is what keeps a returns bench moving without a knife.

Four Liners, and When You Need Each

A corrugated wall is three layers: a liner on each face with a fluted medium bonded between them. The liner carries the print and the sustainability claim, and four of them cover almost every job.

Printing corrugated board

Four Presses, and What Each Cannot Do

Your artwork decides the route, and the route decides what the print can carry: a logo on kraft, a photograph on a laminated sheet, a different name on every box. Each press below carries the job it should not be given.

  • Flexographic

    1–4 colors, in-line

    Kraft liner, a logo or a short line of copy, and volumes where the plate has already paid for itself several times over. It is the only route that prints on a rough board face at speed.

    Not forPhotographs and gradients. A flexo plate carries a logo, not a picture.

  • Offset Litho

    Sheet, exact spot color

    Detailed graphics and a Pantone that has to land on the chip. The printed sheet is laminated to the board afterwards, which is how a corrugated carton gets a photographic panel.

    Not forShort runs. The plate and wash-up are the same whether the run is 500 or 50,000.

  • Digital

    No plate, no setup

    Short runs, a seasonal change, a market test and the prototype that has to be in hand this week. Each box can carry a different name from the next at no extra cost.

    Not forThe cheapest unit rate at volume. Above a few thousand pieces, a plate route overtakes it.

  • Screen

    Specialty ink and texture

    A heavy pass of ink on a specialty box, and effects that a press cannot lay down at all.

    Not forA long run. Every unit passes a screen by hand, and the labor is in the handling.

What holds the product

Three Board Fitments, and What Each One Does

The right size stops a product sliding around inside the carton. A fitment stops it moving at all, and movement is what breaks corners and edges. Each one folds from the same sheet as the box, adding nothing to the material list.

  • Dividers and cells

    Slotted board that holds a set upright and apart, cut to the product’s own footprint rather than a standard cell size.

  • Die-cut fitments

    A shaped cradle pressed from a flat board blank, holding a product or several at a fixed spacing.

  • Corner and edge pads

    Extra board where the weight lands, which lets the outside of the carton take the impact rather than the contents.

Before the quote

Where the Money in a Carton Goes

Most of a corrugated quote is settled by the blank rather than by a unit rate, which is why the lines below are mechanics rather than prices.

  • 01

    Board Is Bought by the Ton

    A mill sells liner and medium by weight, and the weight of a carton is the size of its blank. The cheapest route to a lower unit cost is a smaller blank, before any conversation about a cheaper liner.

  • 02

    The Flute Grade Moves the Price Most

    A step down a flute grade changes the board weight, the take-up of medium and the stack rating together. It is the largest line in a corrugated quote and the easiest to get wrong in the direction of over-specifying.

  • 03

    The Die and the Plate Are Paid Once

    Both are cut or made for the first run and then held. A reorder carries neither, and a design change on the same carton carries the plate only.

Testing

Tested Before It Ships, Not After

A carton that fails in a parcel network fails in front of a customer. Four checks stand between the print run and the pallet, and each check produces a figure rather than an opinion.

  • 76 cm

    Drop

    Face, edge and corner drops on a packed carton at the ISTA 3A height, on the floor of the lab rather than on a loading dock.

  • All axes

    Random vibration

    The same carton run on a vibrating table, which is what finds a score line that would open across a parcel network.

  • Stack load

    Compression

    Pressed to the load the stacked pallet pattern implies, with the figure printed on the report rather than left as a pass or fail.

  • AQL 2.5

    Lot inspection

    The sampling plan, the defect count that would fail the lot and the date of the report, all issued with the shipping papers.

What Buyers Ask Before a Corrugated Run

01What is the minimum order for a custom corrugated box?

500 cartons, and the wholesale band runs from there to 500,000 units. Tooling sets the floor — the cutting die and the print plate are made once and kept for the reorder — and under 500 units they cannot be spread far enough for the unit rate to be worth quoting.

02Single wall or double wall — how do I decide?

By what sits on top of the carton, not by what goes inside it. The flute table above carries a safe top load and a line on the run it suits for each grade; send the pallet pattern and the packed weight, and the choice follows from those two figures rather than from preference.

03ECT or Mullen — which number should my specification carry?

ECT, unless a customer of yours has specified otherwise. Edge crush test measures the board’s resistance to crushing on edge, which is the load a stacked carton actually sees, and it is the number that survives a change of liner without the spec having to be rewritten. Mullen burst measures puncture resistance, and it still belongs on a spec for a heavy, sharp-edged part. Both figures come off the mill certificate for the lot, and either can be carried on the packing list for the run.

04White liner or litho lamination — which costs less?

A white liner under the print, and it is the route to ask about first. It costs less than laminating a printed sheet to the board, and it keeps the carton fully recyclable, which the laminate does not.

05Can an order mix flute grades and print routes?

Yes, and most do. A range usually has a carton carrying the weight and several carrying the branding, which argues for a high flute grade on the shipper and a fine grade on the retail box. What cannot be mixed is the board booking: each grade is a separate mill line, each route is a separate setup, and each is quoted as its own line rather than discovered on the invoice. Two grades in an order is normal; six is worth consolidating first.

06How flat does a corrugated carton ship?

An RSC travels folded on its glue line at roughly its own depth plus a few millimeters — a carton 300 mm deep ships around 20 mm thick. Trays, wraps and lid-and-base sets travel flatter still, and units are strapped and corner-protected inside the shipper.

07What do you need from me to price a run?

Four things: the packed product size, the packed weight, the quantity, and whether the print covers a panel or the whole carton. Size and weight settle the flute and the blank; quantity settles the tier; the print panel settles whether a plate is involved. A photograph of the product and a line on how it is packed is usually enough to close the last gaps.
