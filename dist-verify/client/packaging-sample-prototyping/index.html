What Each Sample Looks Like, and What It Proves

Every sample is built to the same structure, so the step between the four is the material and the finish — not a different box. Here is what each one shows and the risk it takes off.

01 · in-screen model

3D Sample

See the box before a sheet is cut.

What it looks like

  • Rotate it and open it from any angle
  • No physical sheet is printed
  • A link to send for client sign-off

What it validates

  • Structure reads correctly on screen
  • Proportions and openings look right
  • Catch a dieline error before tooling
02 · unprinted rigid box prototype

White Dummy

Hold the structure, check the fit.

What it looks like

  • No print — board, flute and fold only
  • Corrugation and tuck flaps visible
  • Magnetic closure, tabs and assembly on hand

What it validates

  • Product fits, never floats or jams
  • Assembly is easy to repeat on a line
  • Wall thickness and weight feel right
03 · digital printed carton prototype

Digital Colour Sample

See the artwork on the real board.

What it looks like

  • Digitally printed artwork
  • Registration crosses and cut edges visible
  • Whether the colour reads dull or true

What it validates

  • Artwork sits inside the safe lines
  • No trapped text or bleeding
  • Screen colour holds on coated board
04 · production sample vs mockup

Production-Line Sample

The real material, the real process.

What it looks like

  • Same material, print and finish as the run
  • Foil, lamination and emboss in place
  • A transit-tested version beside it

What it validates

  • Foil and finish hold in the hand
  • The box survives the courier route
  • Cost per unit is locked before bulk
Packaging sample approval

Sign It Off Like a Real Factory

The approved sample is the reference the whole run is built to. Notes and a signature replace a “yes, looks good” — so the line, the printer and QC all work from the same sheet.

  • Colour referencePantone / CMYK proof stapled to the sheet
  • Board & fluteThe exact grade the line will run
  • Dimensions & dielineLocked to the signed template
  • Finish mapFoil, varnish, emboss — spot for spot

Should the sample drift from the spec — colour variance or a material mismatch — we rework the sample or credit the order, so the pack you approve is the one that ships.

Material & durability

Test the Material, Not the Mockup

A sample is built to the material the order ships — corrugated, greyboard, kraft or coated carton, plus eco and food-contact grades and finishes like foil and emboss. That makes it a true proxy for the run, not a stand-in.

  1. 01

    Weight-bearing

    Rated to the stack height the box will meet in a warehouse or store.

  2. 02

    Drop & impact

    Dropped flat, on edge and corner — the product stays clear of the board.

  3. 03

    Thickness & rigidity

    Caliper and stiffness measured against the specified board, not assumed.

  4. 04

    Print & colour fastness

    Ink adhesion and colour held after rub and light, before the first use.

  5. 05

    Environmental simulation

    Humidity and temperature cycling — the conditions the route adds.

FAQ

Before You Order a Sample

The questions that come up before a proof. Structure and artwork detail live on their own pages.

QWhy order a packaging sample?

A sample tests what a render can’t — transit durability, true colour, and how easily a team assembles the box. A mistake found here costs a sample; the same mistake in production costs a run.

QHow does the sample process start?

The design file or a brief is checked first — print and material feasibility, a suggested build, then the first sample is cut.

QIs the sample the same as the production order?

The approved sample is built to the board, print and finish the line will run, and it becomes the reference the whole run is checked against. The production-line sample is the version that has been through a simulated transit test, so it proves the box on the route before the first bulk run.

QAre samples revised if they are not right?

Yes. A sample is revised against feedback on fit, colour or finish until it reads the way you want, then it is signed off for the run.

QWhat if the sample doesn’t match the spec?

Where variance is on the supplier side — colour drift or material not matching the order — the sample is reworked or the order credited, so the approved spec is the one the run is built to.
