US sourcing usually narrows to three routes — scale and private label (Poly-America, Inteplast, Berry), branded retail distribution (Reynolds, through Hefty), and a wide can-liner catalog with verifiable recycled or compostable content (Aluf, Novolex).

Poly-America makes Husky contractor bags and industrial film, and is a competitor in the same category. The comparison below fixes that attribution and marks which companies are built around can liners versus retail carrier bags.

## How We Screened These Manufacturers?

Every company below cleared the same five checks before it made the list:

- **Can liners or trash bags are a primary line**, not a catalog add-on bolted onto other packaging.
- **A published spec sheet exists** with gauge (mil or micron) and seal type.
- **Any food-contact or compostability claim** maps to a named standard: FDA food-contact resin, ASTM D6400, or BPI.
- **Private label or custom print** is offered at a MOQ a mid-size buyer can actually place.
- **US production or distribution** reaches multiple regions.

Three exclusions decide whether this list is even the right starting point:

- **Volume under one pallet.** Below that, a local distributor usually beats a factory-direct minimum.
- **A certification the maker cannot produce on request.** A logo on a website is not a certificate.
- **A custom width, gauge, or closure outside the catalog.** That is a tooling conversation, not a supply-list pick.

**Gauge to application.** Liners are sold by thickness, and that number decides where a bag survives:

- **6–10 micron (HDPE):** light office and lobby waste; thin, crinkly, low leak resistance.
- **0.5–0.9 mil (LDPE):** breakroom, restroom, and light kitchen duty.
- **1.0–1.5 mil:** standard commercial and food-prep loads.
- **1.5–3.0 mil / contractor:** construction debris, sharp edges, puncture risk.

**Failure modes to check before ordering.** Leaks usually trace to a flat seal instead of a star seal, punctures trace to gauge and resin blend, and odor traces to closure type and liner-to-can fit. Request a sample in the gauge you intend to buy.

**US versus overseas OEM.** US factories win on lead time and freight risk for pallet freight; an overseas OEM wins on unit cost at high volume but adds ocean transit and customs time.

The list below is a US shortlist — if landed cost is the deciding factor, compare an overseas quote before committing.

## Conclusion

The right supplier depends on which of three routes matches the job. Scale and private label point to Poly-America, Inteplast, or Berry. A recognized consumer brand on shelf points to Reynolds and Hefty.

A wide can-liner catalog with verifiable recycled or compostable content points to Aluf or Novolex. The adjacent makers — Four Star, Advance Polybag, Superbag — are the right pick only when retail carrier bags are also on the order.

The screening checks matter more than the ranking: confirm gauge and seal type from a spec sheet, ask for the certificate behind any green claim, and match MOQ to real volume.
