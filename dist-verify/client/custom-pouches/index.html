Six Decisions Inside One Printed Pouch

Every pouch is the same handful of layers and the same four seals. What gets specified is which of these six details are built into yours — and each is a line on the quote.

  1. 01

    Hang hole

    An optional euro slot punched through the header, letting the pouch hang on a pegboard rail instead of standing on a shelf.

  2. 02

    Tear notch

    A notch cut into both edges just above the zipper. The first open is a straight line across the pouch rather than a torn corner.

  3. 03

    Resealable zipper

    A micro-textured grip that closes under thumb pressure. The profile is chosen for the fill — fine powders need a wider track than beans.

  4. 04

    Degassing valve

    A one-way valve that lets a fresh roast release carbon dioxide without letting oxygen back in. The pouch settles instead of ballooning.

  5. 05

    Heat-seal seam

    Wide, even seals on every edge, tested in-line. A weak seal is the one failure a pouch cannot hide — a leak shows up as a soft pouch, not a visible tear.

  6. 06

    Bottom gusset

    The fold that decides whether a pouch stands up. Its depth sets the fill volume and the shelf footprint together.

Three Ways to Build the Same Pouch

The same 250 g of coffee fills any of these. What changes is how much film goes in, whether it stands by itself, and how much of it the shelf can see.

Flat Pouch

Two film panels, sealed on four edges

Front and back, joined on four edges. The least film per pouch, the largest uninterrupted print area, and the lowest carton weight per unit.

Choose it whenIt is a refill, or it ships inside an outer carton anyway

Stand-Up Pouch

Two panels plus a folded bottom gusset

The gusset unfolds into a base and the pouch stands unaided, keeping its shape as the contents settle.

Choose it whenThe pouch has to sell itself off a shelf

Side-Gusseted Pouch

Two panels, two side gussets and a base

Two side gussets plus a base lift usable capacity 30–50% on an unchanged footprint, and give the pouch four more printable faces.

Choose it whenThe fill is bulky and the shelf width is fixed

Process

From Brief to Pallet in Four to Six Weeks

Five stages, each with a deliverable you can hold, and no stage released until the stage before it is signed off.

  1. Day 1

    Quote

    A reference pouch, a photograph or a dimensioned sketch is enough to open the quote. The print cylinder cost is shown separately from the unit price, and the two can be weighed against each other.

  2. Day 1–3

    Dieline

    Drawn around the fill weight, sent as a layered vector PDF that artwork drops straight into. A 3D mockup comes back within 24 hours of the brief.

  3. Day 3–10

    Printed sample

    A printed, sealed and filled pouch arrives by courier. The zipper is felt, the seal is squeezed and the Pantone is checked in daylight rather than on a screen.

  4. Day 10–20

    Production

    The run goes across the press and the laminator. Incoming film is checked on arrival, seals are tested in-line, and finished pouches are pulled for AQL inspection before packing.

  5. Week 4–6

    Freight

    Pre-made pouches ship in cartons. Where the pouches are filled on a form-fill-seal line, printed roll stock ships instead, wound to the core diameter that machine takes. FOB, CIF or DDP, named on the quote.

Film

Four Films, Chosen by What Has to Stay Out

Each of these blocks something different — light, oxygen, moisture or grease — and that is what decides which one a fill gets.

PET (Polyethylene Terephthalate)

High clarity and tensile strength with a good barrier against gases and moisture. It is the printed outer layer on most pouches we run.

Goes toSnack food, hardware, cosmetics

Kraft with Foil Barriers

Kraft’s matte, natural surface over a thin foil layer. The foil does the barrier work; the kraft does the talking.

Goes toPremium coffee, gourmet food, technical goods

EVOH Barrier Laminate

An oxygen barrier with no metal in it, so the pouch stays see-through. The alternative when a full foil layer is more barrier than the fill calls for.

Goes toFresh food, snacks, anything that has to show through

Compostable Film

A film made from plant stock rather than oil, engineered to break down in a garden bin and not only in an industrial one.

Goes toOrganic food, tea, artisanal goods

Print methods

The Press, the Face and the Ink

Three decisions taken in order, and the three that decide whether a run repeats cleanly: which press runs the job, which face the ink sits on, and how the colors are mixed.

The press

  1. 01

    Flexographic

    Ink laid from a plate on a cylinder. The cheapest route once the artwork stops changing and the run is long.

  2. 02

    Digital

    No plate and no cylinder — the file goes straight to the press. For short runs and artwork that changes between orders.

  3. 03

    Screen

    Ink pushed through a mesh and laid on thick, for a solid or a metallic that has to cover completely.

The face

A pouch wall is four layers. Which one the ink goes into is the whole difference between these two.

The ink

Spot Colour

A single ink mixed to a Pantone reference per color, so a brand color repeats exactly on the next run. It is the default on every pouch we quote, and the reason a reprint matches the run already in the market.

Finish

What the Shelf Sees and the Hand Feels

The print decides what a pouch says. The finish decides how it reads across a shop floor, and what it feels like in the hand.

Matte

A flat, non-reflective surface that keeps fine type readable straight under shop lighting.

Gloss

High contrast and depth. The default when the artwork is photographic.

Metallic Foil

A metallized layer or a hot-foil stamp for a mirror finish on a logo or a single band.

Soft-Touch

A velvet-matte laminate. It changes how the pouch reads in the hand before the label is read.

Questions

What Buyers Ask Before Signing Off a Pouch

Five questions the sections above do not already answer.

01Can you match a pouch we already use?

Send a sample. The film is measured for thickness and layer count, the gusset and the seal width are taken off the pack itself, and the dieline is drawn from that rather than from a written description. A printed sample comes back to compare side by side before anything is committed.

02Which films count as recyclable under EU PPWR?

Mono-material structures — an all-PE or an all-PP pouch — are the ones aligned with the direction PPWR sets for packaging. Laminates that combine PET, foil and PE are not, which is why the film call is made with the destination market in mind. Tell us where the pack will be sold and the structure is proposed against that rule.

03Can you print barcodes, QR codes and batch numbers?

Yes, as part of the artwork rather than as a label applied afterwards. A code is printed to the same tolerance as the rest of the design, and where the code changes between batches the print file is versioned so an outdated file cannot reach the press.

04How do payment terms and milestones work?

A deposit against the print cylinders, then the balance against the shipping documents, by T/T. The printed sample sits ahead of both as its own smaller milestone, so the bulk run is never released against an unapproved proof.

05What happens if a batch does not match the approved sample?

The approved sample is the reference and it stays on file. A batch that measures outside it is stopped before packing rather than shipped, and the pouches are remade or the film reprinted — nothing leaves until it matches what was signed off.
