The options

Compare All Custom Options, Material to Handle

Five choices decide how a bag feels, prints and carries — pick each one to see the options and a real sample.

The fabric decides how a bag feels, how long it lasts and the story it tells. Cotton brings a soft premium hand-feel, canvas adds durability, nylon and polyester shrug off the weather, and jute and recycled fibre carry a natural, compostable story.

Shape decides how a bag packs and carries, and size decides what it holds. A flat bag folds flat to save freight, a gusset adds depth for bulky goods, and the small-to-large range matches the product to the channel.

A logo either carries a brand or does nothing. Choose the method that suits the artwork and the board — screen print for flat colour, embroidery for a tactile mark, embossing for a raised relief, and foil stamp for a metallic highlight. Approve a printed proof before any run.

The finish separates a commodity bag from a premium one. A matte keeps it quiet, a gloss lifts the colour, soft-touch feels expensive in the hand, and an oil- and water-resistant coat protects the print. Pick the surface that matches the shelf and the price point.

How a bag opens, closes and carries decides how often it gets used. Twisted paper handles give a classic grip, cotton webbing feels softer, a magnetic closure snaps shut cleanly, and a zipper seals the contents. Choose the hardware that fits how the customer holds it.

FAQ

Before the First Order

The questions that come up before a first order.

1Which artwork file is needed to start?

A print-ready PDF, or an editable AI or EPS from the designer. Bleed, safe lines and spot colours are checked before the first print, so the proof matches the file.

2Can colours be matched to a brand standard?

Yes. Colour is matched to a Pantone reference or an approved printed proof, and held run to run so a reorder matches the first batch.

3Is a new design kept confidential?

A Non-Disclosure Agreement is signed before any artwork is shared, and the design is used only for the order it was made for.

4What happens if a batch is not right?

Where variance is on the supplier side — colour drift or material that does not match the order — the batch is reworked or the order credited.

5What payment terms are offered?

A deposit secures the order and the balance settles before shipment, with terms for repeat buyers available.
