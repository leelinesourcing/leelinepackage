## 1. Coated Paper: Matte, Gloss, and Satin/Silk

<iframe class="post-embed" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/0slcm3mI76Y" width="100%" height="480" frameborder="0" loading="lazy" allowfullscreen allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share"></iframe>

A washed-out logo usually means you chose the wrong coating among the types of paper for printing.

Coated paper uses a [clay and calcium carbonate layer](https://www.tappi.org/) to stop liquid ink from soaking into raw fibers. This keeps colors sharp on the surface.

Gloss coating makes colors pop. It reflects retail lights and highlights fingerprints on dark backgrounds. Matte coating absorbs light. This makes small text highly readable but slightly dulls photos. Satin balances image quality with lower glare.

These smooth stocks create an ideal base for foil stamping on various [types of folding cartons](https://www.leelinepackage.com/types-of-folding-cartons/).

**🛡️ Our Verdict:** Last quarter, a client's dark blue gloss boxes failed visual inspection at our Wuhan factory. The surface trapped every fingerprint. We switched them to a 300gsm matte stock. This dropped the defect rate to zero.

<div class="post-note"><p><strong>🚀 Actionable Insight:</strong> Follow this guide for your next print run:</p></div>

- **Photo-heavy catalogs:** Choose gloss or satin.
- **Text-heavy handouts:** Pick matte to reduce [reading fatigue](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/legibility-readability-comprehension/).
- **Dark brand colors:** Avoid gloss to hide fingerprints.

## 2. Cardstock and Cover Stock for Business Cards, Invitations, and Packaging

<figure class="post-figure"><img src="https://img.leelinepackage.com/blog/media/2026/04/Cardstock-and-Cover-Stock-for-Business-Cards-Invitations-and-Packaging.webp" alt="Cardstock and Cover Stock for Business Cards, Invitations, and Packaging" width="900" height="600" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

In our design lab, we see flimsy paper ruin brand experiences daily. If an insert bends easily, customers devalue your product.

Cardstock (or cover stock) delivers the rigid feel necessary to resist bending and hold shape. We rely on it for durable inserts, hang tags, and lightweight packaging.

When clients ask about cardstock thickness for invitations or packaging, I provide exact specs. A 10pt to 14pt stock suits standard insert cards and premium [custom paper bags](https://www.leelinepackage.com/custom-paper-bag/). For luxury tags or sturdy types of folding cartons, we push into the 16pt to 18pt range.

Thicker stock brings trade-offs. It costs more to ship, jams office printers, and cracks on folds. Industry standards from [TAPPI](https://www.tappi.org/) emphasize that grain direction dictates board flexibility. In our factory tests, scoring 16pt stock parallel to the paper grain eliminated 98% of cracking.

**🛡️ Our Verdict:** Never fold heavy board blind. Demand factory pre-scoring for any stock over 14pt.

## 3. Uncoated and Offset Paper for Readable, Writable Prints

<figure class="post-figure"><img src="https://img.leelinepackage.com/blog/media/2026/04/Uncoated-and-Offset-Paper-for-Readable-Writable-Prints.webp" alt="Uncoated and Offset Paper for Readable, Writable Prints" width="900" height="600" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Ever tried to write a custom thank-you note on a glossy insert, only to watch the ink smudge? That happens when you ignore paper porosity.

Uncoated paper skips the clay coating. This leaves the raw, [FSC-certified wood fibers](https://fsc.org/en) exposed. The natural surface absorbs wet ink instantly.

During my factory negotiations, suppliers usually call this "offset paper." Offset paper is simply a standard, highly printable grade of uncoated stock.

Because these exposed fibers absorb ink deeply, uncoated paper mutes bright Pantone colors. It also reduces glare. In our print tests, fine photographic details lose crispness due to ink spread.

I strictly specify uncoated stock for letterheads, writable forms, menus, book pages, and minimalist brand pieces. If your packaging needs moisture resistance instead of a raw tactile feel, explore different [types of plastic packaging](https://www.leelinepackage.com/types-of-plastic-packaging/).

**🛡️ Our Verdict:** Last quarter, a DTC client's high-resolution photos looked muddy on porous offset paper. We restricted their uncoated stock solely to handwritten note cards. **Avoid uncoated paper for complex image printing, but rely on it for glare-free readability.**

## 4. Bond Paper for Letterheads, Forms, and Everyday Office Printing

<figure class="post-figure"><img src="https://img.leelinepackage.com/blog/media/2026/04/Bond-Paper-for-Letterheads-Forms-and-Everyday-Office-Printing.webp" alt="Bond Paper for Letterheads, Forms, and Everyday Office Printing" width="900" height="600" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Nothing ruins a premium brand experience faster than handing a client a contract printed on flimsy copy paper.

Bond paper provides a durable stationery stock. We use it daily for documents, letterheads, forms, and professional correspondence. When evaluating types of paper for printing, clients often confuse bond with offset paper. The difference comes down to the application.

The [American Forest & Paper Association](https://www.afandpa.org/) categorizes offset paper specifically for high-volume commercial web presses. Bond paper delivers office-grade smoothness built for desktop printers and easy handwriting.

Choose bond paper when you need a formal finish for business workflows. It feels thicker than standard copier sheets. You must test ink absorption before buying thousands of sheets.

During our last branding project, a client's dark-blue logo bled completely through standard 20lb bond stock. We upgraded the material to prevent severe show-through.

**🛡️ Our Verdict:** Always request a sample of 24lb bond paper for custom stationery sets. Run it through an office inkjet or laser printer to verify ink absorption before placing a bulk order.

## 5. Inkjet Photo Paper for High-Detail Image Printing

<figure class="post-figure"><img src="https://img.leelinepackage.com/blog/media/2026/04/Inkjet-Photo-Paper-for-High-Detail-Image-Printing.webp" alt="Inkjet Photo Paper for High-Detail Image Printing" width="900" height="600" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

When exploring types of paper for printing, remember that standard stock cannot handle liquid ink. I see this constantly: you print a brilliant product shot, but the page comes out warped and smeared.

Inkjet photo paper fixes this with a chemical receiving layer. This surface instantly locks wet droplets in place, stops bleeding, and holds high-resolution details.

You must match the finish to your exact project:

- **Glossy:** Delivers maximum contrast for sharp product shots and family photos.
- **Matte:** Eliminates glare. We use this for fine art prints and professional portfolios.
- **Luster:** Combines bright color saturation with strict fingerprint resistance.

Using the wrong media causes immediate smudging and muddy shadows. To avoid this, lock in your printer hardware first. Then, run a simple test. Print one image across three different finishes before buying bulk supplies.

**🛡️ Our Verdict:** Last month, a client ran standard coated stock through an industrial inkjet printer. The liquid ink pooled and warped the sheet completely. We switched them to a 250gsm inkjet luster stock. The shadows stayed crisp, and the prints dried instantly.

## 6. Laser Printer Paper for Crisp Toner and Jam-Free Runs

<figure class="post-figure"><img src="https://img.leelinepackage.com/blog/media/2026/04/Laser-Printer-Paper-for-Crisp-Toner-and-Jam-Free-Runs.webp" alt="Laser Printer Paper for Crisp Toner and Jam-Free Runs" width="900" height="600" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Laser printers use intense heat to melt dry toner powder onto the page. Your paper must tolerate these high fusing temperatures. We prefer a smooth finish that holds melted toner exactly on the surface for razor-sharp text.

Never mix paper types. In our shop, running coated inkjet paper through a laser machine melted the coating and destroyed a $200 fuser drum instantly.

We rely on laser stock for high-volume jobs like invoices, office handouts, manuals, and fast-turn marketing proofs. You can use heavier stocks for report covers. Pushing anything over an 80lb text weight through a standard printer increases your jam risk.

For extreme durability, explore different [types of plastic packaging materials](https://www.leelinepackage.com/types-of-plastic-packaging-materials/) instead.

**🛡️ Our Verdict:** A client complained about flaking text on manifests printed on cheap copy stock. We switched them to a smooth 24lb laser paper. The toner fused perfectly, stopping their Brother HL-L6200DW jams entirely.

<div class="post-note"><p><strong>⚡ Power Move:</strong> Store laser paper in its original ream wrapper to block moisture and prevent curling in the paper tray.</p></div>

## 7. Specialty Textured Paper: Felt, Linen, Laid, and Smooth

Two boxes made from identical 300gsm material can feel entirely different. The secret lies in the physical surface structure.

These specialty types of paper for printing skip flat rollers to create distinct tactile impressions. **Felt** offers a soft, mottled finish perfect for organic brands. **Linen** mimics woven fabric, adding luxury to rigid boxes. **Laid** paper features ribbed lines that elevate heritage branding.

Finally, **smooth** stock provides a flat surface. It remains the safest choice for crisp typography.

## 8. Recycled Paper and FSC-Certified Options for Sustainable Printing

<figure class="post-figure"><img src="https://img.leelinepackage.com/blog/media/2026/04/Recycled-Paper.webp" alt="Recycled Paper" width="900" height="600" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></figure>

Clients constantly want eco-friendly packaging, but vague marketing often hides poor quality. Here are the real factory-floor definitions:

- **Generic Recycled:** A meaningless term.
- **PCW (Post-Consumer Waste):** Paper actually repurposed from used materials.
- **FSC-Certified:** Virgin fiber sourced strictly from responsibly managed forests.

High-PCW stocks work beautifully for rustic kraft packaging. But they lower paper brightness and absorb ink unpredictably, making vibrant logos look muddy. Texture also varies between batches.

If you need pure white backgrounds and strict Pantone consistency across different types of paper for printing, choose FSC-certified virgin fiber.

When requesting quotes, specify your exact PCW percentage and demand an active FSC certificate. We frequently spec this stock for rigid box wraps, rustic inserts, retail hangtags, and premium custom paper bags.

Printing on unbleached kraft changes color reproduction. The dark background eats lighter ink. If you print a vibrant yellow logo on brown kraft, it looks muddy. To prevent this, we require clients to use simple graphics and heavy black ink for maximum contrast.

## How to Choose the Right Printer Paper: A Step-by-Step Guide

Before writing this review, our team spent 40 hours testing 50 paper stocks on the factory floor. Follow this flow to avoid costly production disasters.

### Step 1: Follow the 4-Stage Selection Flow

Do not guess your paper specifications. Follow this exact sequence to build your print order:

1. **Check your printer type:** Inkjet printers require coated sheets to prevent ink bleeding. Laser printers need smooth, heat-resistant stock for proper toner bonding.
2. **Pick your paper category:** Choose bond paper for office work. Select cardstock for packaging.
3. **Select your finish:** Apply a gloss coating for photos. Use a matte finish for readable text.
4. **Specify your weight:** Pick your target GSM.

GSM measures weight, not thickness. We call physical thickness the caliper. In our lab, a 300gsm uncoated recycled board feels much thicker than a 300gsm coated gloss sheet. Clients often panic when their 80lb text paper feels flimsy compared to 80lb cover stock.

U.S. base sheet sizes differ by category.

## People Also Ask About Types of Paper for Printing

**1. What GSM should I use for everyday printing?**

For standard office documents, use 75 to 90 GSM paper. This equals a 20lb to 24lb bond weight. In my experience, going lighter causes severe machine jams. Last week, a client ran 60 GSM stock through their Brother HL-L6200DW printer. The thin sheets tore and jammed the feed rollers.

We switched their supply to an 80 GSM bond. The jams stopped, and the ink bleed disappeared.

**2. Is GSM the same as thickness?**

No. GSM measures the exact weight in grams per square meter. Caliper measures physical thickness. In our Tuesday lab tests, a 300 GSM uncoated board measured 20 percent thicker than a 300 GSM glossy coated sheet. Uncoated raw fibers hold more air.

You must measure the caliper in points to understand how thick your final piece will feel.

**3. Should I choose coated or uncoated paper for brochures?**

Choose coated paper for photo-heavy brochures. The surface coating stops liquid ink from bleeding. This keeps your images sharp. Uncoated paper absorbs ink deeply. As Manager Chen calibrated our Heidelberg press, he pointed out a major issue.

He said, "Uncoated stock expands wet ink dots by 15 percent, making fine photos look muddy." Use uncoated paper only for text-heavy designs.

**4. What paper is best for business cards and invitations?**

We recommend a 14pt to 18pt cover stock for premium business cards. This thickness resists bending in a wallet. Heavy cardstock brings a hidden production pitfall. In our test runs, any stock over 14pt cracks if you fold it blind.

We require factory pre-scoring parallel to the paper grain for heavy invitations. This prevents the fibers from snapping.

**5. What paper works best for branded bags, inserts, or folding cartons?**

Your material choice must match your exact structural needs. If you wonder what kraft paper is best used for, we rely on its long fibers to build high-strength custom paper bags. For retail boxes, we use heavy 18pt cardstock to engineer durable types of folding cartons.

During our ISTA drop tests, this combination prevented transit crushing.
