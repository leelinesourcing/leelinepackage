The corner

A Corner That Lands in the Same Place, Every Time

Four corners come out of the run aligned: no bare board along the edge, no printed panels stopping short of each other, and nothing that reads as improvised the moment the lid comes off.

  • 01

    What the 90° V-cut does

    The groove is cut at the corner angle, and the board is positioned by machine rather than by eye, so the wrap folds on the same line from the first box of a run to the last.

  • 02

    What it prevents

    The wrap riding up the board until a sliver of grey shows along the edge, and printed panels that should meet at the corner never quite touching.

  • 03

    Why it slips elsewhere

    A V-groove cut a fraction off the corner angle, or a wrap laid down before the board is fixed — so the paper finds its own line instead of the intended one.

Customization

Built Around the Product, Not a Stock Size

Four decisions are settled before the press runs, and each is drawn against a real object rather than a catalogue page.

01

Structures

Bespoke die-cut outlines, divided compartments and lids that slide instead of lift. The shape is drawn from the product’s own measurements, so nothing has to be packed around a compromise.

02

Inserts

The insert is what holds the item still and sets the height it presents at. Foam, board or moulded pulp, cut to the exact object rather than padded out with filler.

03

Surface

Wrap and finish are settled as a single decision, before the first plate is made. What the surface feels like in the hand is chosen at the same time as what it looks like on a shelf.

04

Fittings

Ribbon pulls, leather handles, magnetic closures, elastic bands and window film. These are the details a buyer meets first, so they are never an afterthought.

Board

The Core Under the Wrap, Chosen on Purpose

A rigid box is a greyboard core finished with a wrapped surface. Those two choices decide the weight in the hand and how the print reads.

Grey Chipboard

Recycled pulp pressed into a dense core — the reason a rigid box keeps a shape a folding carton cannot.

Best forThe structural core of every rigid box

Duplex Chipboard

Grey on the back and white on the face, so a printed wrap needs no separate liner.

Best forPrinted wraps on a tighter budget

SBS C2S

Bleached board coated on both faces; the smoothest sheet for fine line detail and a soft-touch lamination.

Best forCosmetics and premium retail

White Kraft

Unbleached fibre with a clean white face, for a natural look without the brown.

Best forEco ranges and natural goods

Questions

The Answers Before You Write

01Can I send a sketch or a reference box instead of a production file?

Yes — a dimensioned sketch, a reference box or a print-ready file all work. What matters is the product dimensions and how it should sit inside.

02Can you match my brand colour exactly?

Yes. Pantone matching is used across every element, and supplying the colour codes up front gives the closest result between the screen and the ink.

03How fast can a rush order run?

5–7 business days plus shipping, on a rush slot. The fee depends on the quantity and how complex the build is.

04What happens if a batch does not match the approved sample?

The signed sample is the reference for the whole run. Where the variance sits with us, the batch is remade or the order credited.

05Can the boxes be delivered straight to our warehouse or a 3PL?

Yes. Boxes leave the plant by sea or air, and can be routed to a port, a distribution centre or straight to your own dock.

06Do you keep the tooling on file for a reorder?

The die and the plate set stay against the artwork, so a second run of the same structure and print does not start from the drawing board again.
