How We Print on Tinplate — five switcher layouts

Five structurally different ways to present the six finishes, each with its own image. All are live on this page. The dashed boxes are the image slots — the dashed shape says what the photograph is. Layouts 1, 2 and 4 are tab patterns; 3 is native <details> and 5 has no controls at all. All of them work without a mouse.

Layout 1

Index rail — a list you pick from, one stage on the right

Six finishes stacked as a numbered index. The chosen one turns forest green and its image and copy appear beside it. Compact, reads like a contents page, and it stays on one screen — nothing below the fold moves.

Layout 2

Filmstrip — six image frames in a row, one stage beneath

Every finish shows its own picture at once, so the range is visible before anyone clicks. The selected frame gets a brass rule and the full-size shot appears below with the copy. Best if the photographs are the selling point.

Layout 3

Accordion — six full-width rows, the open one shows its picture

Each finish is a row with a number and a +. Opening one drops its photograph and copy in beneath, at full width, so the image is large. Nothing is hidden behind a tab, and it needs no JavaScript. Tallest of the three.

Layout 4

Overlay — copy on the picture, tabs under it

One wide frame carries the image and the finish name and copy sit on the photograph itself, over a scrim along the bottom. The six names run as a tab strip directly beneath, the active one underlined in brass. The controls and the picture read as a single object, and the section keeps a fixed height.

Layout 5

Scroll rail — six complete cards, no tabs to click

Nothing to switch: the six finishes sit side by side as complete cards — picture, name and copy together — in a rail you scroll sideways, each one snapping into place. It shows everything at once in a single strip and needs no JavaScript. The trade is that a reader who never scrolls sideways only ever sees the first card.
