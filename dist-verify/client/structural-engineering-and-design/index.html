1 · Structure

Pick the Structure That Matches the Channel

A shelf pack and a mailer protect differently. The right choice fits the sales channel — not the one that looks best in a render.

Folding carton (tuck-end / auto-bottom)

Retail shelf + DTC

Light, everyday SKUs in steady rotation

Cheapest per unit, flat-packs for storage, auto-bottom erects fast on the line.

Lid & base (rigid)

Gifting + retail

Gifts, premium and fragile items

Thick greyboard walls, four strong corners, and a reusable keepsake the buyer keeps.

Magnetic-closure gift box

Gifting + DTC

2 · Material

Engineer the Board, Not the Buzzwords

Strength is measured in ECT — the load a corrugated edge takes before it buckles. Sized to the stack the box must hold, so a heavy SKU never ships on a light board and a light one never pays for a heavy one.

Single-wall flutes (E · B · C)

Board depth is the trade-off: a thin flute prints crisply and ships light, a tall one cushions and resists crushing. Choose the shortest depth that still protects the route — the spec for each is in the table below.

BC double wall

Two flutes bonded face-to-face roughly double the cushioning and crush resistance for about 2 mm extra thickness. The cost is a heavier pack and a little more board — worth it only when the route demands it.

Solid greyboard (rigid)

No air pockets — a dense card that keeps its shape. It adds almost no cushioning of its own, so a fitted insert does the protecting; what you gain is a hard, reusable shell customers keep.

FSC & food-contact grades

Certified and food-safe grades, sourced on request. They don’t alter the structure — they open retail and food channels that won’t accept uncertified board.

Humidity: corrugated loses roughly 30% of its crush strength in damp air. For a box that must stack in a humid DC, a moisture-resistant treatment and a step up in ECT cost less than the damage they prevent.
3 · Protection

Stop the Product Touching the Box Wall

Most damage isn’t a crushed carton — it’s the product moving inside it. The insert is what stops that.

Fragile & glass

A die-cut corrugated or EVA insert pockets each item with a 3 mm+ air gap off each wall — so a 1 m ISTA drop leaves the bottle intact.

Cosmetics & bottles

Card slots or a partition separate each unit, so product never touches product — and stays recyclable with no foam.

Electronics & heavy

Double-wall BC board, corner protectors and a moisture-resistant tape keep a full pallet stack from distorting.

Light & durable

A well-fit carton with no foam and no filler — the smallest volume that still tracks through the line.

4 · Testing

Prove the Box With ISTA, Not Guesswork

Tested to the protocol the ship route actually uses, then recorded on the engineering note — so a buyer sees evidence before the first unit ships.

ISTA-1A / 2A / 3A

Drop

Dropped flat, edge and corner from 30–76 cm depending on package weight, to confirm the insert keeps the product off the box faces.

Pass: Product functional + box intact after the test drop.

ISTA-3A random vibration

Vibration

1–3 hours of random vibration that mirrors truck / air transfer, checking the insert does not migrate during a real journey.

Pass: No movement of the item inside its pocket.

Compression (ECT-linked)

Stack & compression

Hold 1.4× the tallest warehouse stack for 24 h; a 44 ECT single wall carries ~55 lb per edge, BC double wall ~95 lb.

Pass: No collapse at the load the DC will actually use.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

QWhat’s needed to start the structure?

A photo or sketch, the product size and weight, and the channel — retail shelf, DTC or Amazon. Rough dimensions are enough; the dieline is drawn around the product to those figures.

QWhich structure suits the product?

It depends on fragility and the channel. A folding carton wins for everyday DTC SKUs; a rigid lid-and-base or magnetic box for gifts and premium; a mailer for Amazon size tiers. Send the channel and get one recommendation plus a comparable option.

QCan the box be guaranteed to survive shipping?

No supplier can guarantee a courier, but structure is the biggest lever. The box is engineered to an ISTA protocol matched to the route, then evidenced with drop, vibration and stack tests before the run — damage returns typically below 1%.

QHow does structure change cost?

Mostly in freight and damage, not the unit price. A tighter dieline cuts dimensional weight, a die-cut insert removes foam and loose fill, and the right flute stops breakages — usually a double-digit % off landed cost per unit.

QWhat materials and certifications are available?

Corrugated in E, B, C and BC double-wall, plus solid greyboard for rigid boxes. ECT is sized to the stack the box holds; FSC-certified and food-contact grades are sourced on request for retail compliance.

QIs the dieline the customer’s?

Yes. The dieline and tooling belong to the customer; a master stays on file so a reorder is a reprint, not a redesign.
