Why a tin

Why Choose a Tin Box

A folding carton can carry a better photograph for a third of the unit cost. These four are the reasons to pay for the metal instead.

  • One-Piece Drawn Body

    A tin body is a single piece of metal pressed out of a flat blank and closed on a rolled seam. There is no glue line, no folded corner and no score for a courier to break along.

  • Blocks Light and Moisture

    Metal stops light and moisture by itself. Board gets there with a liner or a film bag inside it; a tin arrives already closed, which is why tea and coffee go in one.

  • No Label to Peel

    The artwork is in the surface of the metal rather than on a label stuck to it. There is no edge to lift, no laminate to delaminate and no second operation to cut a print down to size.

  • Kept and Reused

    A tin gets kept. It becomes the sewing box, the spice tin, the place the good pens live. That second life is the cheapest repeat impression a brand can buy.

Lid types

The Five Tin Lid Types We Make

The lid is the only part of a package a buyer physically handles before the product, and it is the part a smooth-walled pack has no equivalent for.

  1. 01

    Friction slip lid

    Use it forMints, lip balm, tea sachets, cards

    Presses onto a straight wall with the seam of air escaping as it seats — the cheapest closure to tool, and the closure that still opens with cold hands.

  2. 02

    Gasket seal

    Use it forTea, coffee, spices, biscuits, supplements

    A food-grade silicone ring set into the lid channel, bearing on the body rim. This is the closure that makes a tin airtight — a friction lid and a hinge do not.

  3. 03

    Concealed hinge

    Use it forGift sets, first-aid, sewing kits, tobacco

    The lid stays attached, so it cannot be dropped or set down wet, and the outer face stays unbroken for a wrap-around print.

  4. 04

    Rolled screw thread

    Use it forPowders, creams, waxes, lip balm

    Closes in stages rather than in a single push, which suits a fill opened and closed many times over its life.

  5. 05

    Tamper band

    Use it forCosmetics, pharmacy, supplements

    A band bridges lid and body and tears on the first opening, so the break reads at a glance from across a counter.

Food safety

Food-Grade Linings for Every Fill Type

An acidic or fatty fill will attack an unlined wall, and the fault shows up as a taste rather than as a leak. The lacquer follows the fill rather than the budget.

  • FDA 21 CFR 175.300

    The US route for resinous and polymeric coatings on food-contact metal.

  • EU 10/2011

    The European route, with a migration test run on the finished tin rather than on the print alone.

  • Lot certificate

    The coating batch and the migration report travel with the shipping papers, so a retailer auditing the claim has the document in the box.

Your fill decides the lacquer

Only fatty and acidic fills change the answer — both attack an unlined wall. Everything else ships on the standard food-contact lacquer.

Printing and finishing

How We Print on Tinplate

The artwork goes on before the metal moves at all, which lets a tin carry a full wrap where a board carton only gets a panel. Pick a finish to see what it is.

  • Six-color offset litho

    Artwork printed on the flat sheet before forming, so the full wrap lands on the metal in one pass. Six colors plus a varnish is enough for a photographic panel on the lid and a second design on the body.

  • Matte or gloss varnish

    Applied on the press: the tin reads dull to the touch or reflective, with no lamination to lift at an edge. A matte face hides fingerprints and scuffs; a gloss face deepens dark ink and photographs well.

  • Emboss and deboss

    A logo raised or recessed into the metal on the same pass as the print. Tactile, and impossible to peel off. The depth is held on the die, which keeps the raise identical from the first tin to the last, and on the reorder.

  • Spot UV

    A raised gloss mark over a matte base, for the detail that has to catch a light on a shelf. It is the cheapest way to give one element of a design a surface of its own.

  • Metallic and pearl inks

    Gold, silver and pearl laid as ink rather than as a foil, which keeps the tin recyclable as steel. Foil is pressed onto flat board; on a formed tin the ink route is what holds its edge.

  • PVC window

    A cut aperture closed with clear film, for a fill that has to be seen before the lid comes off. The film goes in on the same pass, which means the window adds no separate assembly step.

FAQ

Custom Tin Box FAQ

01How do the tins ship, and what does that do to freight?

Nested and palletized. An empty tin is a container with nothing in it, which means tins stack inside each other and a pallet carries several times the number its footprint suggests. Lids are banded separately wherever the nesting would score the print, and the pallet is corner-protected for the sea leg.

02Can one order carry several shapes?

Yes, and most ranges do. A gift set with three components usually carries three tins, and each new outline is its own pair of dies. What cannot be shared is the metal booking: each wall grade is a separate coil and each outline a separate setup, and both are quoted as their own line rather than appearing on the invoice afterwards.

03Can the inside of the tin be printed?

Yes, and it is worth doing where the lid comes off entirely and the inside face is on show. A printed interior means the ink has to sit under a food-contact lacquer, which adds a coating pass and is quoted as its own line. On a fill that is not food, the interior can be printed directly with no coating over it.

04What happens if a lot fails inspection?

It does not ship. The check runs against AQL 2.5 before the tins are palletized, while the dies are still set on the line, so a failing lot is reworked or remade there rather than argued about after it has crossed an ocean. The inspection record ships with the lot.

05How long does a reorder take?

A reorder skips everything up to the press. The dies are already cut and the artwork already signed off, so it is the production stage alone — printed, drawn, formed and lidded — and it books into the next free slot on the line rather than behind a new tooling set.

06What do you need to quote a run?

The fill, the width and the height you want, the quantity, and whether the artwork covers the lid or the whole body. The fill settles the lining; size and quantity settle the wall and the tier; the print area settles whether a second die is involved. A photograph of the product beside a ruler usually closes the last gap.
