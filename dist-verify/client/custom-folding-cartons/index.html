How it runs

Drawn, Sampled, Printed and Glued Under One Roof

A carton is judged in the hand, not on a proof, so the structure is signed off folded and filled before tooling is committed. Nothing is trucked between vendors, and no batch waits on another shop’s queue.

  1. 01

    Prepress and Dieline

    The cut file is drawn or checked against the product dimensions before a die is made.

  2. 02

    3D Mockup

    A mockup of the finished carton goes out within 24 hours, drawn to the dieline.

  3. 03

    White Dummy

    A white dummy lands in 5 days, for checking the fit in the hand before anything is printed.

  4. 04

    Printed Sample

    A printed production sample follows in 7–10 days, on the board and finish of the run.

  5. 05

    Press

    Chosen on run length and the detail the artwork has to hold.

  6. 06

    Cut, Glued and Packed

    Steel-rule dies cut and score on the same pass, then cartons are folded, glued, counted and bundled flat.

Questions Buyers Ask Before Ordering

01What is the smallest order for printed folding cartons?

500 units per structure and per artwork. Below that, the die and the plate set-up stop making economic sense, and a short run is better served by a stock carton with a printed label.

02Will the cartons run on our existing packing line?

That is what the dieline is for. Send the line speed, the feed width and the magazine capacity, and creasing and board weight are set so the carton feeds and closes without an adjustment.

03Can folding cartons be printed on the inside?

Yes. Inside print, a coloured liner and a printed insert are all available. An interior panel is the part a customer sees at the exact moment the carton opens.

04What if a batch does not match the approved sample?

The signed sample is the reference for the whole run. Where the variance sits with us, the batch is remade or the order credited.

05Can one order carry several sizes or artworks?

Each size needs its own die and each artwork its own plate set, so a mixed order is quoted as the sum of its parts. Keeping to a single size and a single artwork puts the unit price at its lowest.
